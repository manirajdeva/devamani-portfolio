Trekking Journal / Netravathi Peak Trek

Netravathi Peak Trek

Samse, Chikmagaluru, Karnataka 4,987 ft Moderate
DifficultyEasy-Moderate to Moderate
Round Trip12–16 km
Max Altitude4,987 ft
Trail Time5–8 Hours
Trip Duration3 Days

Netravati Peak is trekked from Samse, in the Kalasa area near Chikmagaluru — a 3-day trip out of Bengaluru with the actual climb taking up the full middle day.

Day 1 — Bengaluru → Chikmagaluru

Left Bengaluru early (5:00–6:00 AM) and drove via Hassan and Belur to Chikmagaluru, stopping for breakfast en route — the road journey takes up most of the day. Checked into a homestay in Chikmagaluru by the afternoon, with the evening left relaxed: a walk around town, a coffee estate visit, a sunset viewpoint or the local market, depending on time.

Night stay: Chikmagaluru.

Day 2 — Netravati Peak Trek

Left very early from Chikmagaluru toward Samse, near Kalasa, where the trek begins. The trail moves through forest, a stream, a waterfall area, shola grasslands, and open ridges before reaching Netravati Peak — one of the more rewarding transitions in the Western Ghats, from dense forest into wide-open grassland ridgelines.

At the Summit

Views stretch out toward the Western Ghats, the Kudremukh region, shola grasslands, cloud-covered valleys and the surrounding ridges. Worth taking the time to sit with it rather than rushing back down.

"The climb is difficult only until you stop looking at the distance and start enjoying every step."

Started the descent with enough daylight left to comfortably reach the base, then returned to Chikmagaluru/Kalasa for the night.

Day 3 — Chikmagaluru → Bengaluru

After breakfast, began the return journey — either a relaxed drive back via Belur and Hassan (stopping at Belur if time allowed), or a slower morning around Chikmagaluru's coffee country before setting off in the afternoon. Reached Bengaluru by evening, depending on traffic and stops.

Trek Tips

  • Carry leech-proof socks or salt if trekking during or after monsoon.
  • Start early from Chikmagaluru — the trail alone can take 5–8 hours depending on route and pace.
  • Don't rush the summit — the forest-to-grassland transition and the ridge views are worth lingering over.
