My Inspirations / Nims Dai — Nirmal Purja

Nims Dai — Nirmal Purja (1983–2026)

Mountaineer Ex UK Special Forces Nepal
Who He Is

The Man Behind Project Possible

Nirmal "Nims" Purja was a Nepalese mountaineer and former Gurkha soldier who served with the UK Special Forces before turning to high-altitude climbing full-time. He was widely regarded as one of the greatest mountaineers of his generation, known as much for his physical limits as his mindset.

In 2019, Nims set out to climb all 14 of the world's 8,000m peaks — a feat that had previously taken mountaineers close to a decade — in under seven months. He finished in 6 months and 6 days, smashing a record many considered impossible.

Key Achievements

A Career of Firsts

Beyond Project Possible, a record sheet that reads like a list of "impossibles" turned real.

Milestones

  • 🏔️ First winter ascent of K2 (January 2021) — led the Nepali team that summited K2 in winter for the first time in history, without supplemental oxygen.
  • ⛰️ Everest, Lhotse & Makalu in 48 hours (2019) — summited three of the world's five highest peaks back-to-back in a single window.
  • 🎖️ Special Boat Service (SBS) — the first Gurkha soldier ever to pass selection into the UK's elite SBS, after 16 years of military service.
  • 👑 MBE — appointed Member of the Order of the British Empire for services to mountaineering.
  • 📖 Guinness World Records — held the record for the fastest ascent of all 14 of the world's 8,000m peaks.
  • 🚁 Mountain rescues — involved in multiple high-altitude rescues during his own expeditions, including on Annapurna and K2.
  • ✍️ Beyond Possible — his memoir detailing the journey from soldier to record-breaking mountaineer.
  • 🏢 Founder, Elite Exped — runs his own expedition company guiding others up the world's highest peaks.
In Memoriam

Lost on Broad Peak

Nims died on July 30, 2026, at age 43.

What Happened

An avalanche struck Broad Peak (8,051m) in the Karakoram range, Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan, during an expedition. Purja was among ten mountaineers caught in the slide; his body was later recovered by rescue teams.

He passed doing the thing that defined him — pushing into the world's highest, hardest places. His records stand, his documentary and memoir remain, and the mindset he modeled outlives him. Rest in peace, Nims Dai.

Why He Inspires Me

Impossible Is Just an Untested Limit

What draws me to Nims isn't just the record — it's the mindset behind it.

He treated an "impossible" goal as a logistics and willpower problem to be solved, not a limit to be respected. That's a mindset I try to bring to my own treks and to the bigger goals I set for myself, on the trail and off it.

His documentary, 14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible, is a reminder that most of the limits we accept are the ones we've placed on ourselves — and that discipline, preparation and sheer refusal to quit can move that line much further than we think.

What I Take From His Story

  • 🏔️ Big goals are made of small, disciplined steps repeated without excuses.
  • 🧠 Mindset and preparation matter as much as fitness.
  • 🤝 Even solo-looking achievements rest on a team working together.
  • 🚫 "Impossible" is often just a label no one has tested properly yet.
